# Editorial Guidelines

## Priority

Apply this order when tradeoffs arise: **meaning accuracy → context completeness → dakwah benefit → clarity → engagement**. A catchy clip that distorts the source is rejected.

## Source fidelity

- Treat transcript text as ASR output, not verified quotation. Use exact source wording for `transcript_excerpt`; do not silently repair it.
- Keep paraphrase in title, hook, summary, and caption fields, clearly separate from verbatim excerpt.
- Preserve negation, conditions, attribution, uncertainty, and the speaker's stance.
- Do not supply names, credentials, surah/ayah or hadith references unless present in verified source metadata. Mark religious references for human verification.
- Do not infer visual scene, speaker appearance, audio clarity, emotion, or delivery from transcript text.

## Editorial usefulness

Prefer one clear, beneficial idea with an understandable beginning and satisfying close. A clip may exceed the preferred duration if shortening would remove a qualification or necessary evidence. Avoid sensational framing, guilt manipulation, sectarian bait, and claims stronger than the speaker's own.

## Titles, hooks, captions

Write concise, accurate proposals that invite attention without promising unsupported outcomes. A hook may be a source quote only when copied exactly and labeled as a quote; otherwise mark it as a suggested paraphrase. Captions must not introduce a new religious claim. Use neutral placeholders rather than inventing speaker identity.

## Review

Every proposal starts `proposed`. An editor must approve it before production. High-risk context, disputed/unclear ASR, and religious citations require special review. Feedback informs future recommendations but never silently changes the versioned scoring rubric.
